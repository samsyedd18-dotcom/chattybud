NOHAY

A Noha is a form of elegiac poetry recited by Shia Muslims to mourn the martyrdom of Imam Hussain and his companions in the Battle of Battle of Karbala. Nohay are especially recited during the Islamic month of Muharram, particularly in gatherings called majalis. Key Features: Express grief, sorrow, and remembrance. Describe the suffering of Imam Hussain (A.S.) and the Ahlul Bayt. Recited in a rhythmic and emotional style. Often performed collectively, with people beating their chests (matam) as a sign of mourning. Nohay play an important role in keeping the message of sacrifice, justice, and truth alive in Shia Islam.

Manqabat

A Manqabat is a devotional poem or song written in praise of the Ahlul Bayt (the family of Prophet Muhammad) or respected Islamic saints. Shia Muslims commonly recite Manqabats in praise of: Imam Ali Imam Hussain Bibi Fatima Purpose of Manqabat: To express love and devotion. To highlight the virtues, bravery, knowledge, and spirituality of the Ahlul Bayt. To strengthen faith and spiritual connection. Unlike Nohay, Manqabats are not mournful; they are usually joyful and full of admiration.

Shia Islamic Scholars

Shia Islamic scholars are religious leaders who study and teach Islamic knowledge based on the teachings of the Quran and the Ahlul Bayt. Some well-known Shia scholars include: Allama Muhammad Baqir al-Majlisi – A famous scholar known for compiling Bihar al-Anwar, a large collection of hadith. Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini – Leader of the Islamic Revolution in Iran and an important Shia jurist. Ayatollah Ali al-Sistani – A leading contemporary Shia authority (Marja) based in Najaf, Iraq. Role of Shia Scholars: Interpret Islamic law (Fiqh). Guide people on religious matters. Teach in Islamic seminaries (Hawza). Preserve and spread the teachings of the Ahlul Bayt. Conclusion Nohay and Manqabat are important parts of Shia religious culture that express grief and love for the Ahlul Bayt, while Shia scholars play a vital role in preserving Islamic teachings and guiding the community.
